Children’s health issues linked to screen use (ages 0–7)

Associations from observational studies, not proof of cause
Health issueEvidenceWhat the research foundSource
Overweight / obesityStrongExcessive screen time associated with overweight/obesity in toddlers and preschoolers (meta-analysis).Li et al., 2020
Shorter sleep durationStrongExcessive screen time associated with shorter sleep in toddlers and preschoolers (meta-analysis).Li et al., 2020
Poorer sleep qualityModerateJapanese study of children under 5: more screen time went with shorter, poorer sleep.Japan study 2019, cited 2025
Developmental delayModerateHigher screen time at 24 and 36 months predicted lower developmental scores at 36 and 60 months (β −0.08, −0.06); no reverse effect.Madigan et al., 2019
Language delayModerateReported association with excessive screen use in young children.Li 2020; Italian Soc. Pediatrics 2026
Myopia, eye strainModeratePooled odds ratio 1.07 per extra hour/day in cohort studies; children and adolescents, not only ages 1–6.Zong et al., 2024
Unconsolidated sleep, injuryLimitedAssociations reported in individual studies of infants, toddlers and preschoolers.Li et al., 2020
Attention, executive function, emotionsLimitedReview of 78 articles links high screen use to these problems; mostly broader pediatric ages.Italian Soc. Pediatrics 2026
No country-level prevalence of these health problems for ages 1–6 was found, so none is shown. Ask a pediatrician about an individual child.
Sources: PMC8259964, PubMed 31334169, NSCH 2024, OECD reports (2018–2025), WHO 2019, JAMA Pediatrics 2019 and 2022, IJERPH 2020, BMC Public Health 2024. “Derived” = 100% minus the reported share. Single data points are plotted at the middle of their reported age band.
