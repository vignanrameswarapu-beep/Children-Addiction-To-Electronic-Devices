# Child Screen Addiction Monitoring System (Ages 1–6)

The Child Screen Addiction Monitoring System is an AI-powered application designed to monitor, analyze, and help manage the usage of electronic devices among children aged 1–6 years. It focuses on identifying excessive screen time and unhealthy digital habits caused by the use of multiple electronic devices, including smartphones, tablets, televisions, laptops, and computers.

The system collects and analyzes screen-time information from different devices to provide parents and guardians with a consolidated view of a child's digital activity. It helps identify prolonged device usage, frequent screen exposure, and usage patterns that may affect a child's sleep, physical activity, attention, and overall development.

**Key Features:**

- **Multi-Device Monitoring:** Track screen usage across smartphones, tablets, televisions, laptops, and other connected devices.
- **Screen-Time Analysis:** Calculate the child's total daily screen exposure across multiple devices.
- **Usage Pattern Detection:** Identify frequent usage, prolonged sessions, and excessive screen-time patterns.
- **AI-Based Insights:** Analyze usage data and highlight potentially unhealthy digital habits.
- **Parental Dashboard:** Provide parents with a centralized dashboard to understand their child's device usage.
- **Screen-Time Alerts:** Notify parents when predefined screen-time limits are exceeded.
- **Usage History:** Maintain daily and weekly reports to monitor changes in screen habits.
- **Parental Recommendations:** Suggest healthier digital routines, offline activities, outdoor play, and screen-free periods.

**Objective:**

To help parents understand and manage children's exposure to electronic devices during early childhood by providing centralized multi-device monitoring, intelligent usage analysis, and practical recommendations for developing healthier digital habits.

**Target Users:** Parents, guardians, childcare centers, and early childhood educators.

*Note: The application identifies screen-use patterns; it does not independently diagnose addiction or developmental disorders.*
